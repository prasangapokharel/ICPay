*Nepal flash floods — glacier collapse leaves hundreds dead*

> Al Jazeera · Aug 28, 2026

A *glacier collapse* in the Himalayas triggered catastrophic flash floods through Nepal's mountain valleys on *Wednesday*, killing hundreds and leaving more than *1,900 people missing* across Nepal and Tibet.

*Why it matters*
This was not a routine monsoon flood. Scientists say a massive slide of ice and rock into the Lhende River sent a debris-laden surge down the *Trishuli River* — wiping out villages, roads, bridges, and border infrastructure in minutes.

*Driving the news*
• At least *567 confirmed dead* in Nepal; hundreds more missing in China's Tibet region
• Flood hit a *72 km stretch* of the Trishuli in Rasuwa and Nuwakot districts
• Settlements flooded up to *80 metres* above normal river level
• *Gyirong Port* — a major China–Nepal trade gateway — severely damaged
• Bodies and debris carried *240 km downstream*; flood reached Galchhi

*The big picture*
Early reports blamed an earthquake. Seismic monitors later showed the signal came from the *landslide itself* — not a tectonic quake. Warming is melting Himalayan glaciers faster, making these collapses more likely.

A *barrier lake* has now formed where debris dammed the river at the Nepal–Tibet border. Officials warn it could breach and send a *second surge* into valleys already devastated — forcing rescue teams to pause and retreat to higher ground.

*Between the lines*
"If a formed lake suddenly breaches it may cause another flood; it may mobilise the sediments that will hamper search and rescue operation."
— *Anil Pokhrel*, head of Nepal's disaster management authority

*What's next*
• Rescue operations ongoing in Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Gorkha, and Chitwan
• Nepal government assessing *Rs 200 billion* in preliminary damage
• *15 hydropower projects* hit; ~431 MW of capacity offline
• ~*1,200 people displaced*; 668 tourists from 34 countries among the missing

*What to watch*
Monsoon rain is still feeding the barrier lake. Any sudden breach above the Bhote Koshi and Trishuli systems could hit communities — including Timure and Syaphrubesi — that have no buffer left after Wednesday's wave.
